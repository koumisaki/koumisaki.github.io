Kou Misaki

Machine learning researcher · inference-time scaling for LLMs

I'm a Research Engineer at Sakana AI in Tokyo, where I work on inference-time scaling: drawing more capability out of large language models by spending more compute at test time. My first-author work includes UnMaskFork (ICML 2026), which brings tree search to masked diffusion language models; String Seed of Thought (ICLR 2026), a prompting method for diverse, distribution-faithful generation; and AB-MCTS (NeurIPS 2025 Spotlight), a tree search that decides when to go wider or deeper. I also contributed to TAID (ICLR 2025 Spotlight), a knowledge distillation method for compact language models. Before Sakana AI, I was a Language Engineer for Japanese Siri at Apple and a software engineer at Recruit. I received my PhD in theoretical condensed-matter physics from the University of Tokyo, where I was a JSPS Research Fellow.

Research

My research asks how language models can turn extra inference-time compute into better answers. I work on search over generation paths, from tree search with autoregressive models to unmasking strategies for diffusion language models; on sampling that stays diverse while remaining faithful to a target distribution; and on the reasoning and coding tasks where these methods pay off.

Publications

  1. UnMaskFork: Test-Time Scaling for Masked Diffusion via Deterministic Action Branching

    Kou Misaki, Takuya Akiba

    ICML 2026

    Treats unmasking in diffusion language models as a search tree; deterministic branching with Monte Carlo tree search beats existing test-time scaling baselines on coding and scales well on math.

    arXiv · blog

  2. String Seed of Thought: Prompting LLMs for Distribution-Faithful and Diverse Generation

    Kou Misaki, Takuya Akiba

    ICLR 2026

    Asking an LLM to first write a random string and derive its answer from it brings its sampling close to a pseudo-random number generator and makes open-ended responses more diverse.
